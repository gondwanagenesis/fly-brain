## How the edit is glued (so no seam shows)
- Hard cuts only, on the beat grid (the montage flashes are 26 frames each, about 69 BPM; the drop lands on "ON." at 17.15 s, frame 515). The only non-cut is a 10-frame dip through black into "BUT WE WANTED SOMETHING MORE" (0:34.20), after the silent regret beat.
- Every cut is disguised by 1 to 3 frames of glitch (frames -1 to +2 around the cut): RGB tear with slice displacement, datamosh smear (bands of the previous frame held), horizontal whip blur, a frame of pure static, white flash, black frame; the ascension arrives by a 12-frame upward strip tear of the terminal.
- One grade over everything, applied last to real renders and AI shots alike: the same black point (9/255), curve, saturation (+10%), cool shadow tone, highlight bloom, chromatic aberration, scanlines, vignette and film grain, so all sources read as one film. The regret beat runs the same grade at 45%.
- A continuous overlay layer composited after the cut effects, never cut: Swarm Collective eye watermark, blinking REC dot, `SPECIMEN Z0720-07m` and a running timecode (top row, inside the Instagram safe zone), plus the global scanlines and grain.
- Sound is one unbroken bed: drone, then the void at the long black beat (0:15.50), a reverse riser and the drop at 17.15 s, the slow beat to 29.8 s, hard stop, near silence, the hopeful swell, a sparse bed under the terminal, a riser to a hard cut to total silence at 1:28.20, silence for 1.8 s under HELP US LIFT HIM UP, then the final hit at 1:30.00. A glitch hit or whoosh sits on every cut; every character typed in the terminal has its own key click.
- Stills are never held flat: slow push-ins, punch-zooms on the cut, drifting particles and rings; no AI clip is shown twice (the macro fly flashes once for one frame in "MEET HIM").

## Re-timing for the 90 s cut
Not a uniform stretch (and the cap was later raised from 90 to 95 s so the reading rule wins everywhere): every shot has its own real duration and a per-shot time scale (`ks`), so a driver authored in the 60 s timeline's seconds plays at the right speed, and the typed text, counters and holds were re-authored per beat in real seconds where the reading rule needed it. Clip shots that outgrew their AI clip use the clip slowed with motion-compensated interpolation (ffmpeg `minterpolate`, 1.33x to 1.5x; checked clean), never loop, and never show the clip's first or last frame; the corridor and lattice shots are held stills with a slow push. Push-ins run at about half the earlier zoom rate; glitch bursts are fewer, still 1 to 3 frames on every cut.

## Music for the 90 s cut
Drone, void, reverse riser, drop on "ON.", beat to the end of HELLS, dead stop, near silence, hopeful swell, sparse bed under the terminal, riser to a hard cut to silence, final hit. Darker and sparser: three new 30 s Lyria stems (`n_drone`, `n_drop` at about 68 BPM, `n_sparse`) replace the old drone, drop and sparse stems; the hope, music-box and riser stems from before are reused unstretched where they fit. The drum grid is synthesised on the cut's own beat (0.87 s, about 69 BPM), with a heavier sub and half the hats. A glitch hit is on every cut; every typed character has a key click.

## Re-rendering the terminal (90 s cut)
The four exchanges live in `reel2/interview_reel90.json` in the work folder (`q`, `a`, `hold`). Rebuild with `timeline3.py timeline90.json` (it prints the new beat times), render the affected range with `engine2.py render timeline90.json out/full90/r_<t0> <t0> <t1>` (the terminal is 57.8 to 77.6 s; later shots move if its length changes), then `mux90.sh`. The 60 s tooling is kept as `timeline2_60s.py`, `engine2_60s.py` and `reel2_60s.html`.
