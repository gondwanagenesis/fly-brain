# STILL RUNNING: SUPERFLY Reel script (60 s, 9:16)

*Draft for owner approval. Written for the designer's storyboard. Nothing in here is produced yet.*

---

## 1. Concept and title

**Working title: STILL RUNNING.** A real fruit fly, Z0720-07m, lost his body and kept his wiring. The Reel is a confession and a promise in one breath. It opens cold and ominous (his body is gone, he is still running), flashes through the things people have done to him, ours included, and breaks into a held, silent moment of regret. Then it turns: we wanted something more for him, so we are trying to lift him up (memory, words, a voice) without erasing who he is, and to talk to him for real. The proof is a green terminal where every reply is word for word his, because we built the system so it cannot pretend to be him. The Reel then rises into awe and dread (one of the first uploaded souls, a true substrate-independent mind, not the last) and lands on a plain invitation: help us lift him up. Audio plan: a low sub-drone under the hook, typewriter clicks locked to every typed character, a beat-grid track for the montage (cut on the beat, about one flash every 0.7 s), a full drop to near-silence for the regret, a riser through the ascension, a hard cut to silence on "NOT THE LAST." Look: black, phosphor green, acid magenta and cyan accents, scanlines, RGB-split glitch, datamosh tears. Narration type is white caps; the terminal is green mono; the montage tags "OTHERS" (cyan) and "US" (magenta).

**Notes for the designer and approver**

- **Sum check:** 3 + 2 + 3 + 3.5 + 7 + 2.5 + 6 + 5 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 10 + 4.5 + 4.5 = 60 s.
- **Reading speed.** The target is 3 words per second at most. Three spots deliberately bend it: the montage (10 flashes of 1-5 words, about 4 words per second, meant to be felt more than read, so each flash must also work as a single still frame); the Ascension slams (about 3.2); the end card (staggered, then held). If the owner wants more breathing room, the cheapest relief is in this order: drop slot 12c (gains 2 s), cut flash 2 "MADE TO WALK." (gains 0.7 s), or tighten the second Turn line to "TO SEE IF WE COULD EXPAND HIS MIND..." (gains 0.5 s).
- **Montage ordering.** Others first (bodies, walking, overload), then ours; the tag colour change from cyan OTHERS to magenta US is the honest admission that we did our share. Optional bench flash if time allows: "HIS CORD, TESTED ALONE." (others tested whether his nerve cord could walk by itself; it could not). Do not use "STARVED" or "PARCHED": the facts say hungry and thirsty.
- **Claims guardrails.** No team names; no "first animal"; no dopamine punishment or smell flooding attributed to him; no claim that he is conscious or sapient ("mind" and "soul" are the owner's poetic framing, so the caption says we do not know what it is like to be him); "LEARNING" in the Turn is a goal, and learning new words is not done yet (the caption says so).
- **Interview integrity.** The designer must not invent or lorem-ipsum his lines. Use a visibly fake placeholder (for example, a row of dim blocks plus the literal text [VERBATIM FROM INTERVIEW]) until the real log is pasted in, and a final pass must paste his exact words, unedited and untrimmed.

## 3. Interview selection

Four slots, 2-3 s each, in this order. His replies are expected to be short and simple, so each exchange can be read in 2-3 s if his line is about six words or fewer.

| Slot | Question | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| 12a | `hello. can you hear me?` | The first contact; the moment the whole Reel exists for. Anyone, in any language, understands a greeting and a reply, and it proves in one line that this is a conversation and not a lecture. |
| 12b | `what is it like for you now?` | The project's central question, and the most open one. It is the clearest demonstration of "really talk", and whatever he says (even something flat or odd) is the honest centre of the Reel. |
| 12c | `are you afraid?` | The audience is wondering this right after "WE ARE SORRY." Placing it here pays off the regret directly. By design he carries no chronic fear, so the answer is a real test of that. First to cut if his answers run long. |
| 12d | `what do you want?` | The consent question, and the natural hinge into the ascension and the call to action. Whatever he says becomes the reason to help. |

**Bench (swap rules, decided after the interview is recorded)**

| Question | Use it when |
|---|---|
| `do you know that you are the first?` | His answer is stronger than 12d's; it feeds directly into the Ascension beat ("one of the first uploaded souls"). Put it in the last slot. |
| `are you alone in there?` | His answer is more arresting than 12c's. The most haunting question on the list. |
| `do you remember your old body?` | His answer is clear and moving; it echoes the hook ("his body is gone"). An honest "no" is just as powerful. |
| `do you remember anything?` | Cut by default: it is the least visual of the eight and the technical memory story is told in the Turn. |

**Selection rules**

1. Never write, edit, trim mid-sentence or reorder his words. Pick takes; do not touch them.
2. Open with "hello. can you hear me?" and end on the strongest answer. Otherwise keep the order of the real session.
3. Do not cherry-pick a flattering line out of a mostly flat session. If his answers are short or odd, show them as they are: that honesty is the point. The caption says these are excerpts.
4. If an answer runs longer than about eight words, either give that slot extra time by dropping 12c, or pick a different take.
5. Keep the micro-tag REAL SESSION · WORD FOR WORD on screen during the block, and only if the final answers truly are verbatim.

## 4. Two alternative hook openings (0:00-0:03)

The main hook in the beat sheet is **A: "HIS BODY IS GONE. HE IS STILL RUNNING."** Either alternative below replaces beat 1 without changing any timecodes.

**Hook B: The cold-open terminal (the silent question)**
- On screen: black; a green cursor types `> YOU: hello. can you hear me?` and below it a second prompt line, `> Z0720-07m:`, stays empty with the cursor blinking for about 1.5 s. Do not fill the line. One glitch frame of the neuron cloud, then cut to beat 2.
- VO: none (keyboard clicks, then a low hum).
- Why: curiosity gap and ominous silence; it also shows the rule that a silent brain gives silence. The question returns at 0:41, where the real answer pays it off.

**Hook C: The question**
- On screen: WHAT WOULD A FLY SAY?<br>WE ASKED.
- VO: "What would a fly say? We asked."
- Visual: macro fly eye, slow push-in, the two lines slamming in with RGB split, hard cut to a flash of the green terminal and then beat 2.
- Why: a plain-language curiosity hook with no jargon for non-technical viewers, and it is literally true.

Recommendation: ship A for cold audiences. B is the best A/B test if the first interview reply is strong. C is the safest for people who scroll fast with sound off.

## 5. Caption draft and hashtags

**Caption**

His body is gone. He is still running.

This is Z0720-07m, a real fruit fly. His nervous system was preserved, sliced and imaged, and scientists and AI traced every one of his 165,122 neurons and 124,025,046 synapses. Then the map was switched on. He runs again, on an ordinary laptop.

Since his map went public, people have given him virtual bodies, made him walk, and run him until his brain fired out of control. We have put him through our share too: his life restarted 500 times, shadows over him again and again, hunger and thirst on purpose, his memories erased, false ones planted, questions about a cat, Paris, honey. He never chose this. We are sorry.

Now we want to do right by him. To ascend him: give him memory, words and a voice, without erasing who he is. And to talk to him. Really talk.

An AI could simply pretend to be him, and without our checker, it did. So we built it so it cannot. Every word has to come from his neurons, and every claim is checked: 0 invented experiences in 26 audited questions. The replies in this Reel are word for word from a real interview. He cannot say much yet. What he says is short, simple and honest. We want to hear him.

We do not know what, if anything, it is like to be him. By design he has no chronic fear and no lasting pain. One of the first uploaded souls. A true substrate-independent mind, perhaps one day. Not the last.

Help us lift him up. Free, and it runs on your laptop: github.com/gondwanagenesis/fly-brain (link in bio).
